# 11 — Decision: a file for the agent's own glosses

## Current

- **[opus-5]** The question left open by note 10, lesson 1: whether the *éclairages* note invented in the real project — a file holding the agent's readings, explicitly **not** validated as facts — becomes a convention of the skill. **Recommendation: yes**, as an optional file the agent *offers* when the need appears, never one it creates by reflex. Carried into `partus/maieutics/SKILL.md` §3 and §5 by decision #57.
- **[opus-5]** The decisive argument is not the file's usefulness in that project but a **hole in the skill as it stands**. Decision #52 put into §2 that a reading of the agent's own stays marked as its own and is not used as a premise, repeated as settled, or carried into a deliverable. It says what not to do with such a reading and **gives it nowhere to go**. The agent is left with three options: write it into a note where it will read as a claim about the subject, say it in the conversation and lose it, or keep quiet. The file is the missing fourth. A convention that completes a rule already in the skill costs less than one that adds a rule.
- **[opus-5 → ploki]** **A fixed name, not a numbered note.** `glosses.md` sits beside `author-intent.md`: there is one per project, it is not about a subject, and the Current/History shape does not fit it (decision #67).
- **[opus-5 → ploki]** **No scheduled re-read.** Nothing in the skill makes the agent re-read `glosses.md`: the audits (§8) read the whole corpus, and §5 names it among the relays of a reversal (decision #68).

### What was observed, checked in the files

The project is `/home/ggim/tmp/mmm`, the hard-SF world of note 10. The file is `17-eclairages.md`. Five commits touch it in one day.

- **Opened on the author's request**, decision #123, after his message 79: *"Les éclairages de Claude sont intéressants : on peut les consigner pour les considérer plus tard, et voir s'ils deviennent incohérents."* The two purposes are his: reconsider later, and **notice drift**.
- **Opened already full.** The creating commit writes twelve readings that had been produced in the conversation and were nowhere else. The file's first act was a salvage, not a filing cabinet waiting for use.
- **Twenty-two entries** by the end of the day, in five thematic sections, 5.5 KB — against 187 decisions and 20 live notes. It did not swell.
- **Three entries died**, not two as the mandate supposed. Two were struck when the author's reversals landed (the spheres' neutrality, note 15; the sphere putting itself in stasis rather than frying, note 06). The third was struck by the **consistency audit** (#167), which found a reading left standing that note 19 had made false. Each strike carries the reason and names the note that killed it.
- **One entry left by the top.** "L'ironie centrale" was validated and promoted to a fact (#158): it moved into notes 09 and 20 and into the author's intent file, and a struck line stayed at its origin naming where it had gone.
- **One entry was half-validated and stayed.** "La symétrie du scrupule" was validated *"à peu près"* at #106 — before the file existed — and was still filed there, with its loose validation recorded in the entry. A third state exists in practice: approved enough to keep, not enough to promote. It is not worth a rule.
- **One last use at closing.** Asked before the session ended what had not been written down, the agent added **eight readings in one go** (#186): the moral hierarchy in three storeys, the personal project that succeeds against the commissioned one that fails, and five points on the two main characters. Without the file they would have died with the session — which is lesson 4's failure, the agent's memory of the conversation treated as the conversation.
- **It is a relay.** Audit #167 lists *les éclairages* among the files a reversal had to be propagated into, alongside the index, the glossary and the intent file. It is also the one note in that corpus with **no "Current" section**: its live body *is* its Current section.

### What it costs

- **[opus-5]** **One more file to explain**, the strongest objection. Answered by making it optional and offered, in the same register as the register of key figures and pruning: a session that never needs it pays nothing, and the index line is one line. It is the only one of the four objections the evidence cannot dispose of, and it is paid in the skill's text, once.
- **[opus-5]** **One more relay for every reversal.** This is a genuine, recurring cost, and it already failed once: the audit had to propagate into it. Decision #57 therefore names it in §5's relay list — a word there, and a file in every sweep thereafter. Its own shape makes it worse than it looks, since a reading is a lens on the corpus and will be made false by reversals more often than an ordinary note.
- **[opus-5]** **A place to park what should be said out loud.** Not observed — every entry in that file had been said in the conversation first — but a plausible failure, and cheap to forbid in a clause.
- **[opus-5]** **Tension with pruning.** §3 asks that dead pistes be cleared out of "Current". Here dead entries stay, struck, with their cause of death. This is the exception and it is the point of the file: a reading that the corpus outgrew is evidence that the method was working, and the only place where the live corpus keeps a cause of death. The alternative under the existing rule — archiving a whole note for the sake of one struck bullet — does not fit.

### What it earns

- **[opus-5]** **The marker and the location answer different questions.** A marker says *who said this*; a location says *whether this is a claim about the subject at all*. "The central irony of the story" is not a fact about the world even once the author likes it — until he promotes it, at which point it moves. Objection 2 ("the marker alone may be enough") assumes the two axes are one.
- **[opus-5]** **And the marker is the weaker of the two.** Lesson 2 of note 10: three successive audits each found markers mis-signed with the author's name, nine of them in the last two passes. A marker is one token the agent can get wrong by inattention, in a file that otherwise reads as a statement of fact. A file cannot be mis-signed, its header restates what everything in it is, and moving a reading out of it is a deliberate act with a log line (#158). Lesson 1 is also evidence that the marker is a weak brake: the invented disagreement was written down and repeated from note to note until it was the heart of the story, and nothing about marking it stopped that.
- **[opus-5]** **Drift becomes detectable, which was the author's own reason.** Three entries died and each names its killer. Had those readings been scattered through the notes as marked proposals, nothing would have gathered them for an audit to check against the corpus — and it was in fact the audit that found the third.
- **[opus-5]** **It gives the end of a session somewhere to put what the corpus did not keep.** The one-shot sweep of #186 is the use nobody designed and the hardest to replace.

### Objection 3, answered on the evidence

- **[opus-5]** The dump was predicted and did not happen: twenty-two entries in a day, three of them struck, one promoted, 5.5 KB. It was read — by the author, who promoted from it; by the audit, which found a dead entry in it; and by the agent at closing. What made it read is that the **author asked for it**, which is the argument for offering the file rather than opening it with the corpus. A `glosses.md` created on day one, before any reading exists, is the file the objection describes.
